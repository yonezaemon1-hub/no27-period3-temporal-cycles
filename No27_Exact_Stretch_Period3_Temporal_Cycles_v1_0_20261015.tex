\begin{abstract}
We consider the optimization form of stretched temporal graph realization (STGR) introduced by Mertzios et al., restricted to an undirected cycle $C_n$ and period $\Delta=3$.  With one edge-availability phase per period and fastest strict temporal paths, we derive an exact closed-form expression for the optimal multiplicative stretch: $1$ for $n=3,4$, $3/2$ for $n=5$ and even $n\ge6$, and $5/3$ for odd $n\ge7$.  Explicit cyclic labelings provide the upper bounds; short local constraints on oriented walks give the lower bounds, including the exceptional seven-cycle.  Two differently implemented exhaustive checks agree on $3\le n\le9$.  The analytic proof and computational evidence have passed independent review. This is an unrefereed preprint; historical novelty and priority remain unestablished.
\end{abstract}

\section{Model and statement}
Let $C_n$ have vertices $0,\ldots,n-1$ and edges $e_i=\{i,i+1\}$ (indices modulo $n$). Assign each edge a phase $\lambda_i\in\mathbb Z_3$. Edge $e_i$ is usable at all integer times congruent to $\lambda_i$ modulo $3$, with temporal path traversal times strictly increasing. As in STGR\,\cite{mertzios2025}, the duration of a temporal path with edge phases $a_1,\dots,a_d$ is the last traversal time minus the first plus one; this equals
\begin{equation}\label{eq:duration}
T(a_1,\ldots,a_d)=1+\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}w(a_j,a_{j+1}),\qquad
w(a,b)=\begin{cases}(b-a)\bmod 3,&a\not\equiv b\pmod3,\\3,&a\equiv b\pmod3.\end{cases}
\end{equation}
For an ordered vertex pair $(u,v)$, let $T_\lambda(u,v)$ be the smallest duration of either of the two simple directed arcs of $C_n$, and let $d(u,v)=\min\{(v-u)\bmod n,(u-v)\bmod n\}$. Define
\[
\opt(C_n,3)=\min_{\lambda\in\mathbb Z_3^n}\max_{u\ne v}\frac{T_\lambda(u,v)}{d(u,v)}.
\]
The objective and the temporal-graph model are established in the STGR literature; only the cycle/period-three exact classification below is under consideration here. The model follows the strict, earliest-possible traversal convention and the ordered-pair definition of stretch in Mertzios et al.\ (MFCS 2025, Section 2); phases $0,1,2$ are a relabeling of their $1,2,3$.

\begin{theorem}[Exact classification]\label{thm:main}
For every $n\ge3$,
\[
\boxed{\opt(C_n,3)=\begin{cases}
1, & n=3,4,\\
\tfrac32,&n=5\text{ or even }n\ge6,\\
\tfrac53,&\text{odd }n\ge7.
\end{cases}}
\]
\end{theorem}

\section{Upper bounds: explicit labels}
We write phase words $\lambda_0\cdots\lambda_{n-1}$ in cyclic edge order.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:upper}
The values in Theorem~\ref{thm:main} are attainable as upper bounds.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For $n=3$, all distinct vertex pairs are adjacent, so the value is $1$. For $n=4$, use the cyclic word $0101$. For every opposite pair at least one of its two 2-edge arcs has duration 2, so the value is also $1$.

For $n=5$, choose $02101$. All adjacent edge phases differ. The diameter is $2$, and every 2-edge arc has duration at most $3$, so the stretch is at most $3/2$.

For even $n\ge6$, take $\lambda=(01)^{n/2}$. Traversing either direction along any simple arc of $d$ edges, the successive waits alternate between $1$ and $2$. Thus
\[
T\le d+\left\lceil\frac{d-1}{2}\right\rceil\le\frac{3d}{2}.
\]
For $n=7$, take $0210101$. Adjacent phases are different, and every simple arc of $d\le3$ edges has duration at most $1+2(d-1)$. This yields ratios $1,3/2,5/3$ for $d=1,2,3$, respectively.

Finally, let $n\ge9$ be odd, put $k=(n-7)/2$, and use
\[
\lambda=0202121(01)^k.
\]
Its cyclic differences $g_i=(\lambda_{i+1}-\lambda_i)\bmod3$ lie in $\{1,2\}$ and no three consecutive differences are equal. Indeed, the initial cyclic difference pattern (including the junction into the repeated $01$ part) is $2,1,2,2,1,2,2,1,2$, followed, when $k>1$, by an alternating $1,2$ tail; the wrap-around also preserves the property. Reversing an arc replaces every wait $g$ by $3-g$, so the same no-three-equal property holds for directed waits in either orientation. Any directed arc of $d$ edges has $m=d-1$ waits, at most $\lceil2m/3\rceil$ of which can equal $2$, and hence
\[
T\le d+\left\lceil\frac{2(d-1)}3\right\rceil\le\frac{5d}{3}.
\]
Taking a shortest static arc between each pair proves the upper bound.
\end{proof}

\section{Lower bounds}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:lower3/2}
For all $n\ge5$, $\opt(C_n,3)\ge3/2$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Consider two consecutive edges with phases $a,b$. Their 2-edge arc in one direction has duration $1+w(a,b)$, and the reverse 2-edge arc has duration $1+w(b,a)$. The two waits cannot both equal $1$: if $a=b$ they are both $3$, otherwise their sum is $3$. At least one orientation therefore has 2-edge duration at least $3$. For $n\ge5$, the alternative simple arc has $n-2\ge3$ edges and duration at least $3$. Thus some ordered pair at static distance $2$ has fastest duration at least $3$, and stretch at least $3/2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:lowerodd}
For odd $n\ge9$, $\opt(C_n,3)\ge5/3$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Assume a labeling has stretch strictly below $5/3$. For each oriented 2-edge arc, the alternative has $n-2\ge7$ edges, so the 2-edge arc itself must have duration at most $3$. Consequently consecutive edge phases differ; write $g_i=(\lambda_{i+1}-\lambda_i)\bmod3\in\{1,2\}$.

For each oriented 3-edge arc, the alternative has $n-3\ge6$ edges, so the 3-edge arc itself must have duration at most $4$, since $3(5/3)=5$ and durations are integral. Taking the clockwise arc $e_i,e_{i+1},e_{i+2}$ gives $1+g_i+g_{i+1}\le4$. Taking the reverse orientation of the same three edges gives $1+(3-g_i)+(3-g_{i+1})\le4$. Therefore $g_i+g_{i+1}=3$ for every $i$, i.e. $g_i$ must alternate $1,2,1,2,\ldots$ around the cycle. That is impossible on odd $n$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:lower7}
$\opt(C_7,3)\ge5/3$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Suppose a labeling has stretch below $5/3$. Each 2-edge arc must have duration at most $3$, because its alternative has $5$ edges. Hence $g_i\in\{1,2\}$ for all $i$. Since $\sum_i g_i\equiv0\pmod3$, the number of 2's among the seven gaps is either $2$ or $5$. Reversing the orientation of the entire cycle interchanges $1$ and $2$; assume without loss of generality that there are five 2's.

Some adjacent gaps are both $2$ (otherwise there would be at most three 2's). After rotating indices, let $g_i=g_{i+1}=2$. The clockwise 3-edge arc on $e_i,e_{i+1},e_{i+2}$ takes duration $5$; to obtain stretch below $5/3$ for its ordered endpoints, the complementary 4-edge reverse arc must take duration at most $4$. Its waits are $3-g_{i+5}$, $3-g_{i+4}$, and $3-g_{i+3}$, so this requires $g_{i+3}=g_{i+4}=g_{i+5}=2$. These are all five 2's, leaving $g_{i+2}=g_{i+6}=1$.

Now the clockwise 3-edge arc with waits $g_{i+3}=g_{i+4}=2$ also takes duration $5$. Its complementary reverse 4-edge arc has one wait $3-g_{i+6}=2$, so its duration is at least $5$ as well. This ordered pair has static distance $3$ and fastest duration at least $5$, contradicting stretch below $5/3$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
The universal bound $\opt\ge1$ is immediate because no path has fewer edge traversals than static distance. Combine Lemma~\ref{lem:upper} with Lemmas~\ref{lem:lower3/2}, \ref{lem:lowerodd}, and \ref{lem:lower7}.
\end{proof}

\section{Reproducibility and scope}
We supply an exact rational enumeration of all $3^{n-1}$ phase-normalized labelings for $3\le n\le9$ and a separate clock-time simulation implementation of the same enumeration. Their optimal values are respectively $1,1,3/2,3/2,5/3,3/2,5/3$ for $n=3,\ldots,9$. The clock-time implementation also rules out all $42$ phase-consistent $\{1,2\}$-gap sequences on $C_7$ under a hypothetical stretch $<5/3$ by checking both directions of the 3-edge arcs. Infinite-family witnesses are checked computationally through $n=101$. These finite tests \emph{support} but do not replace the analytic proofs.

\paragraph{Novelty limitation.}
The STGR model and objective are prior art\,\cite{mertzios2025}, with subsequent work on possible stretch values and period two\,\cite{meusel2026}. To the best of our knowledge, the specific exact classification for $(C_n,\Delta=3)$ was not identified in the literature examined. However, we do not claim priority; a complete historical assessment remains open.
